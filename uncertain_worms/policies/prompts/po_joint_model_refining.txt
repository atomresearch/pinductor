Generalization principles
-------------------------
Your goal is a **world model**: a compact, general description of how the
environment behaves, not a lookup table of the samples you have seen.

1. **Rules over memorization.** Express conditions on semantic content,
   not on the literal arrays present in the samples.
2. **Cover the space, not the sample.** Enumerate every plausible case the
   environment can produce and decide what should happen in each, even
   when the sample does not exercise it.
3. **Parsimony.** Prefer the shortest rule consistent with the data. Long
   nested branches enumerating specific coordinates almost always overfit.
4. **No episode or step references.** Conditions must depend only on the
   arguments your function receives, not on which sample they came from.
5. **One coherent hypothesis.** When several samples disagree, choose the
   single most general rule consistent with all of them — do not stack
   special cases for each one.
6. **Keep what was already right.** When extending your previous code,
   do not drop a case that already worked. Add the new case alongside.
